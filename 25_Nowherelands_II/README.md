# Nowherelands II

A procedural night landscape that sings back. Walk a 16 km continent of mountain ranges, river valleys, lakes and sea cliffs, find the landmarks, and the music grows with what you discover.

Open `index.html` through any static server (ES modules need `http://`). A seed can be pinned with `?seed=word`; the link in the corner shares the current world. Each seed bakes its own continent in a worker while the intro shows, five to eight seconds on a laptop.

## Controls

| input | action |
| --- | --- |
| W A S D / arrows, Shift | walk, sprint |
| mouse | look (pointer lock) |
| click | touch a thing: cycle a monolith's note, ring a stone, flip time |
| hold click | charge a strike: louder, brighter, bigger ripple |
| R | record the session's audio to a `.webm` |
| gamepad | left stick move, right stick look, A / RT touch |
| touch | left half joystick, right half look, tap to touch |

Stand still for a while and a sprout grows where you rest. Walk into a lake or a river and you wade.

## The land

The world is not noise: it is a landscape evolution model run at load time (`js/world/gen/WorldGen.js`, in a Web Worker).

1. A warped island mask gives coast and coastal plain; two or three tectonic spines give uplift.
2. Stream-power erosion (Braun & Willett ordering, implicit solve) cuts drainage networks into the uplifted ranges, with hillslope diffusion and talus. Hard rock erodes slowly, so it leaves cliffs, gorges and stepped strata; soft rock gives beaches and rounded hills. A coarse pass shapes the ranges, an upsampled fine pass carves the small valleys.
3. Depression filling finds the lakes, from lowland lake districts to cirque tarns high in the ranges.
4. Catchment drainage is rebuilt into a family of rivers (`js/world/gen/Rivers.js`, `ChannelMorphology.js`). Orographic runoff feeds stronger mountain streams, and smaller alpine catchments form headwaters. Hard, confined torrents cut rocky gorges; step-pool streams descend through irregular pools and sills; gravel rivers alternate shallow riffles with scoured pools; lowland channels migrate through broad floodplains. Width and depth vary over several channel widths. The outer bend is deeper, the inner bend builds a sediment shelf, and valley terraces are asymmetric. Each surface cross-section stays level while the river's longitudinal profile descends. Manning roughness varies with the setting, speed follows discharge divided by section area, and delta branches conserve their assigned discharge. Boulders, bedload and gravel clusters belong to the same river data as the channel and its wakes.
5. Where a big river meets a soft, sheltered coast it builds a delta (`js/world/gen/Deltas.js`), modelled on the river-dominated kind (Wax Lake, the Lena, the Mississippi's passes): a lobe of low marshy plain raised out of the sea, its axis the seaward normal of the coast, filling a bay if it sits in one. Across it the river splits the way a mouth bar splits a real one (Edmonds & Slingerland 2007): the channel widens as its jet slows over the bar, both daughters start at the node with that full width and narrow to their own while their centrelines slide apart, and each turns away with a heading that eases in over a few of its widths, so no channel ever has a corner. Adjacent daughters differ by the 25 to 45 degrees the references show (a big river fans into three at the apex), the smaller one turns harder and takes a quarter to two fifths of the flow, widths follow discharge so they shrink with every order, and each channel runs a few of its own widths before it splits again, so the spacing shrinks seaward. Between splits a channel is nearly straight, with a bounded low sinuosity rather than a random walk. The islands between are teardrops pointed upstream; the plain carries a natural levee along every channel and sags into backswamp between them; the lobe's land is the envelope of the channel network; a mouth-bar shoal lies under a metre or two of water past every mouth and a delta front slopes down to the shelf. One parameter spans the arcuate fan whose land fills in between the channels and the bird's foot of levee fingers with bays between. Small streams reaching the sea inside the lobe are carried across it as channels of their own. Every distributary is a river record like any other, starting on its parent's centreline and slipping under its surface for its first metres, so the carve, the water ribbons, the shore map and the vegetation need no special cases.
6. Where things grow is a last pass over the baked grid (`js/world/gen/Habitat.js`), four bytes a cell that the vegetation samples for placement and size and the terrain shader tints the far slopes with, so the pattern the near chunks fill with trees is the one the distant hills already show. Wetness is the topographic wetness index (catchment area over slope) plus a floodplain halo around every river and lake that only reaches ground close to the water's own level, so a valley floor is wet and a gorge rim is not. Trees want soil (thin on hard rock and steep faces), water, shelter from the sea wind, and ground below a tree line that is ragged with noise, lower on the shaded side and lower again near the coast; two scales of noise, a broad biome field and a fine stand field, are sharpened into stands with real edges, and a third cuts clearings a few hundred metres across out of the woods, with sharp edges, so the forest opens into meadows. Placement is a jittered grid thinned by that suitability, so a forest has an interior, an edge with a thicket along it, a few pioneer trees standing out ahead of it, and clearings. The interior is old growth. The world runs at about five times human scale (the eye is 11 m up, the grass 2 to 6 m tall), so its giants are 250 to 350 m tall with buttressed trunks 15 to 20 m through that you cannot walk through, spreading broadleaves in the warm lowland whose great limbs fork three times, and sequoia-like conifers taking over with altitude and toward the tree line, whorls of short branches up a straight bole to a spire. Their foliage is hung along the outer branches as leaf-cluster cards cut out of an atlas painted per seed (overlapping leaves with holes torn in the disc, sprays of needles), so a canopy is a ragged mass with sky through it, lit as one rounded crown by the moon, whitening on top when it snows. Leaves let the moon through (a back-lit term) and the whole tree carries an ambient lift, so a canopy is never black from below. The giants keep to the inland: near the coast the forest is medium leafy trees and bare silhouettes, and the colossal groves stand up toward the mountains. Trees that size are seen from kilometres away, so beyond the vegetation chunks a far layer of blocks out to 3 km carries every giant in a low-detail form built from the same skeleton (the trunk, the main limbs, a handful of big cards), frustum-culled and hidden block by block where the near chunks take over. Bare trees stand in the open ground and along the edges. A slow grove field decides where the colossal old trees stand together, half-size younger forest between the groves and a rare monarch towering over its grove; the wet valleys grow the biggest trees and the edges the smallest, old forest keeps its snags and fallen trunks, the exposed coast stunts the trees and leans them inland, and every tree stands with its own silhouette rather than its chunk's. In the belt below the tree line the trees give way to krummholz, squat and streaming downwind, among dwarf scrub that carries on above the last trees. Grass thins under the canopy, on hard rock and with altitude and grows lush in the wet; reeds line every waterline and spread across the marshy flats, the delta backswamps above all, where the ground is too wet for trees.
7. You spawn on a lowland shore near the largest river's mouth (its delta, when it has one), facing upstream toward the mountains.

The baked height grid is sampled bicubically with procedural close-up relief; river channels, banks and waterfall faces are carved analytically from the river data so streams stay crisp at any distance. The terrain renderer is a quadtree with skirts: 3 m cells underfoot (1.7 m where a river runs), 300 m cells on the horizon, the entire continent always in view under a height fog that pools in the valleys. The sea is a clipmap of rings around the player (2 m cells underfoot, 64 m eight kilometres out) displaced by a sum of Gerstner waves, the trochoidal profile the big engines' water systems use, with flat-shaded facets, a Fresnel-weighted planar reflection instead of a mirror, whitecaps where the surface folds (the Jacobian test an FFT ocean uses for foam), and shallows that are see-through down to the sand. Near the coast a signed distance-to-shore field drives breakers that shoal, break and leave torn foam behind them, and the same wave phase runs a foam sheet up the sand and back; the swell runs up a river mouth and dies of the banks closing in, a river's last reach (its end fade, not merely its height, so a delta's low channels stay rivers to their mouths) rides it and takes on the sea's own shading before it dissolves into it, and the channel fills in to the seabed. Lakes and rivers have their own meshes, with finer geometry near the player and consistent depth shading at both distances. Waterfalls share their lip and pool cross-sections with the channel terrain. Boulders sit on scree, at cliff feet, in rapids, on gravel shelves and around plunge pools; driftwood strands on calm banks.

The new inland water shader reads a GPU snapshot of the actual scenery and riverbed, with coloured absorption through the water, Fresnel reflection, moving caustic light and turbulent foam. It preserves the world's faceted palette while giving deep pools a blue-green body and shallow margins visible stones and plants. A single framebuffer copy supplies refraction, with no additional scene render. Near and far surfaces use the same optical model. Waves use accumulated travel time along the river, so changing velocity cannot progressively tear their phase apart. Rapids carry larger travelling and standing waves; rock wakes and drifting foam share the local current. Waterfall sheets accelerate down the face, deform between their fixed lip and foot, and aerate into impact boils and spray.

Bramble thickets, sedge clumps, reeds and submerged ribbon plants are instanced along the rivers. Water depth, current, foam and elevation determine where each grows. Fast mountain channels remain rocky; slow shallows support aquatic plants and sheltered banks grow denser vegetation. Gravel-bed shading and wet banks tie the water to the terrain.

For the research, architecture, limitations and inspection tour, see [RIVERS.md](docs/RIVERS.md). Add `&rivers=1` to a seeded URL to jump between actual generated reaches and inspect rolling frame-time measurements. **Explore here** returns to ordinary play at the selected river.
Lake coverage follows the connected drainage basin and its full submerged inlet reaches, including tributary backwaters. Outlet exclusions follow the downstream river after it drops below the lake. Disconnected remnants become bank terrain. Small enclosed shoals merge into the basin; substantial islands remain. Inlet channels meet the lake level, retain their terminal position through smoothing, and share an overlapping lake surface without raised banks across the mouth. An explicit feeder channel cuts through the lake rim to the waterfall brow. The same path controls terrain carving and water coverage. Wind waves settle at the join, and narrow feeders retain fine geometry in the distant mesh. Waterfall sheets share the river's lip and pool cross-sections, with denser impact spray and mist. Riffles use varied pool lengths and drop heights, slightly graded pools, and curved crests shared by the water and terrain. Crest displacement is bounded by the neighbouring sections to prevent folded geometry.

Run the river checks with `node --test 25_Nowherelands_II/tests/world/*Test.js 25_Nowherelands_II/tests/world/gen/*Test.js` from the repository root.

## What reacts to what

- **World to music.** Altitude opens the filter, walking speed raises note density, stillness thins everything back to the drone. Snow adds wind and shimmer. The moon's height picks the mode at each key change: lydian high, aeolian low.
- **Music to world.** Note attacks pulse the grid, grass tips and sprouts; bass feeds the lines and the water grid; highs twinkle stars and the aurora. Every spatial note rings a ripple on the ground from where it sounded. The forests carry their own lights: tiny lanterns hang on cords from the branches of the giants, swinging in the wind, their warm glow flickering, swelling with the notes and the bass, and flaring around a note that sounds nearby; fireflies wander around the trunks and under the canopies, blinking in short flashes that come faster when the music moves and all at once when a note sounds close by.
- **Discovery.** Each landmark permanently adds a layer: the spiral unlocks the arpeggio, the hollow stones the bells, the still clock the sub bass, the wanderer the shimmer, the mirrors lengthen the reverb.
- **Events.** Snow, aurora, meteor showers, an eclipse that drops the drone an octave, and the hum.

## Layout

```
js/core        seed, PRNG + simplex noise, event bus
js/audio       engine (voices, reverb, delay, analyser, recorder), scale/modes,
               lookahead scheduler, layers (drone, arpeggio, bells, bass, wind, shimmer), conductor
js/world/gen   world generation: uplift, erosion, lakes, rivers, deltas, habitat, spawn (runs in a worker)
js/world       heightmap sampling, quadtree terrain + shader, height fog, sea / lakes / rivers,
               sky (dome, moon, stars, aurora, meteors), snow, vegetation + boulders, fireflies, sprouts, ripples
js/landmarks   sequencer spiral, octahedrons, time monolith, mirrors, wanderer, interaction + discovery
js/player      pointer lock / touch / gamepad controls, head bob, collisions, footsteps
js/fx          bloom, grain, vignette, chromatic aberration
js/ui          intro, crosshair, toasts, seed link
js/events      rare event director
```
