# Weather

Weather now develops independently of the celestial/music event director. The same world-space fields drive clouds, rain/snow/hail, ground wetness and snowpack, plant motion, water disturbance, light and audio. Snowfall and all ground/foliage snow masks are zero at or below elevation 700. Existing permanent summit caps remain above that minimum.

## Simulation

`js/world/weather/WeatherModel.js` is a rendering-independent, seeded column model over the island plus 4 km of surrounding ocean on each edge. Desktop uses 64×64 columns; touch uses 48×48. It advances at a fixed 0.5-second step, using simulation time (including the time monolith). Results are independent of display frame rate and observer position.

- Semi-Lagrangian advection transports vapor and cloud water with the prevailing wind. Open boundaries and ocean evaporation replenish moisture. A moving broad noise field describes incoming air masses.
- Fair-weather air is deliberately drier, with no minimum cloud-cover floor. A three-seed, 30-minute column sweep on a coarse test island averaged roughly 17% coverage: about 64% of samples below 10% cover, 20% lightly cloudy, and 16% above 45%. These are column samples, not the fraction of the visible sky; distant fronts can still be visible from a clear column.
- Temperature varies with the incoming air, time and height. Terrain gradients projected onto wind provide windward lift and leeward drying. Saturation exchange conserves vapor plus condensate; precipitation removes cloud water. The supply and fallout rates are bounded in long-run tests.
- Rain becomes snow through a smooth temperature/moisture-derived snowline, constrained to 740–1030 world units. A separate 700–770 mountain mask guarantees no lowland snow, even if climate settings are changed. In natural weather, rain, snow and hail partition the precipitation intensity. Survey overrides let each phase be adjusted independently, while retaining the elevation gate for rain/snow.
- Surface wetness and snowpack are persistent reservoirs at grid locations. Snow accumulates only on cold mountain ground, melts gradually, and contributes to wetness. Rendering applies slope and elevation masks at each surface position; moving the camera does not repaint the island.
- Moisture, an instability proxy and uplift nominate convective cells. A cell develops over approximately 100 seconds, has a mature phase, then fades over its final 110 seconds. Cells last 290–390 simulation seconds, travel with upper winds and produce gusts, precipitation and lightning.
- Hail eligibility is drawn once per strong cell at 3.5%, with a 1,200-second cooldown across the world. Hail is confined to the cell's central core and a roughly 36-second mature-stage window. These are artistic pacing choices, not climatological frequencies.

`Weather.js` uploads previous/current pairs of two RGBA8 maps: coverage/precipitation/convection/hail and wetness/snowpack/snowline/ground height. CPU and shader sampling align at texel centres. Every rendered frame interpolates the previous/current maps, cloud displacement, base wind and survey settings using the fixed-step accumulator. Gust phases use the continuous render clock. This adds half a simulation second of presentation latency and removes visible 2 Hz jumps. Local weather is sampled before scene updates. Terrain overhead and cave exposure suppress precipitation at the listener and attenuate sound. Rendering stays independent: each particle is clipped against terrain/roof and water heights at its own position, so outdoor weather remains visible through cave openings. Particle activity considers neighboring weather columns and uses each particle’s elevation to choose rain or snow. Roof maps must cover the particle volume before it is drawn, preventing leaks while teleporting. Exterior streaming stays active within 220 m of cave mouths; deeper enclosed passages retain their streaming optimization.

## Rendering and sound

`Clouds.js` restores the original layered, cel-shaded silhouette and deep violet/red-dwarf palette. Moon-facing edges gain a cool silver-violet tint and sun-facing edges a warm red tint. A bounded alpha (82% for ordinary clouds, rising to 94% in storm cores) allows the sun/moon to remain visible through the layer. Shared simulated cloud water still controls clear skies, scattered cover and fronts. Independently evolving domain warps and detail coordinates continuously deform the procedural noise instead of translating a fixed pattern.

The procedural pass is rendered at up to 60% of the drawing-buffer resolution, capped at 960 pixels wide, then composited behind terrain. Clear weather columns exit before evaluating cloud noise. Reflection cameras evaluate the same layer directly. The survey's Low cloud quality button reduces noise detail from five to three octaves. This is an artistic sky layer rather than the earlier opaque volumetric treatment.

Rain ribbons extend along their actual world-space wind/fall velocity and are then perspective-projected. Their length naturally foreshortens when seen end-on; they do not rotate into constant-length screen streaks as the camera pitches. Particle positions remain world-anchored on all axes. Near-camera and wrapping-boundary fades avoid large streaks crossing the lens or particles popping at the recycling boundary. Snow and hail retain round camera-facing billboards, using the active render camera's basis for reflections. Snow falls slowly with turbulent drift; hail falls faster and has a brief analytical bounce on exposed solid ground only. Water surfaces (sea, lakes and rivers) absorb hail at impact, hiding the pellet through the rebound phase. Terrain and water height samples terminate falls. Up to 64 stable, feathered distant precipitation shafts connect the cloud deck to the terrain. Particle activation has a soft opacity threshold, and shafts keep stable grid locations instead of popping between the strongest rain cells. Surface water rings and wet materials sample precipitation/wetness at their own world positions.

Plant sway, grass and lanterns follow the shared local wind direction and continuous gust strength. Tree shaders convert world wind into each instance’s local orientation, anchor their roots, and cap crown displacement. A nonlinear wind response produces much larger movement in storms; zero wind stops weather-driven sway. Natural storm gusts add 18–36 m/s above the prevailing wind. Existing long ocean waves retain their phase/direction and respond gradually in amplitude; stormy lake/river normals gain additional roughness. Lightning lights the clouds, terrain, water and scene materials independently of viewer shelter; cave rock limits that lighting with its per-surface daylight mask. Thunder is queued by strike distance / 343 and played as a spatial filtered rumble, with distance damping and cave attenuation. Audio delay is measured in real seconds even when simulation time accelerates. Rain, hail impacts and wind have distinct intensity/filter responses.

## Scope and approximations

This is a physically motivated game weather model, not a forecast model or a full 3D atmospheric solver. Temperatures and precipitation are deliberately scaled to the world's compressed mountain elevations and short exploration times. There is no explicit pressure solve, resolved vertical fluid dynamics, detailed droplet microphysics, air-mass inversions/freezing rain, or dynamic river flooding. Cloud base is an artistic deck above the peaks rather than a resolved lifting-condensation-level surface. Small-scale cloud motion and storm morphology are procedural; the cloud artwork uses a layered sky projection, not resolved volumes. Surface reservoirs are coarse and last for the current session; they are not persisted across reloads. Canopies do not individually intercept rain. Wind response in distant vegetation and ocean swell is approximated from the observer's local weather. Thunder uses synthesized noise rather than recorded samples.

## Verification

Run all tests from the repository root:

```sh
node --test 25_Nowherelands_II/tests/world/*.js 25_Nowherelands_II/tests/world/gen/*.js 25_Nowherelands_II/tests/weather/*.js
```

The weather tests cover phase-change conservation, elevation thresholds, phase partitioning, observer/frame-rate independence, persistent accumulation, storm lifecycle/lightning, localized short hail, rare hail eligibility/cooldown and two hours of bounded natural simulation. Additional tests cover per-frame interpolation continuity, fair-weather variety across seeds, independent intensity overrides, calm wind, and stronger continuous storm gusts.

For repeatable visual checks open `25_Nowherelands_II/?seed=umbra&weather=1`. The optional survey has Natural, Clear, Scattered, Rain, Snow, Storm and Hail presets; coast/mountain/cave visits and a sheltered Cave mouth view; view controls (including look down, turn around, and face sun/moon); a manual lightning trigger; sound activation; and cloud quality controls. Sliders control cloud cover, wind speed (0–60 m/s), gust variation, independent rain/snow/hail intensity, storm development, lightning frequency (0–30 per simulation minute), flash brightness, and thunder volume. Thunder volume also affects rumbles already playing. Changes ease in over roughly two seconds. Snow tests require the Mountain view; the Snow preset deliberately suppresses lowland rain. Zero lightning frequency disables automatic survey strikes; the Lightning button remains available. Fixtures override only survey runs; **Natural** resumes the evolving simulation. Frame statistics omit two seconds after preset and view button actions. Normal exploration has no survey panel.

Browser checks on the local desktop at a 1280×720 viewport / 800×450 cloud target showed roughly 95–120 median fps and 8–12 ms p95 in stationary coast, storm, hail and summit views (including an audio-enabled lightning check) after settling. These are whole-scene, vsync-limited observations on this machine, not isolated GPU cloud timings or a guarantee for other hardware. Recheck while moving and on target mobile hardware before raising particle/sample budgets.

The refinement pass also checked slider combinations in-browser: 25% rain / 40% hail at the coast became 70% snow / 40% hail on the summit with the independently selected snow setting; lowland snow remained zero. The storm preset produced about 52 m/s wind with visible plant bending. Clear/scattered, manual/automatic lightning and zero flash/thunder settings ran without browser errors. The full suite passed 53 tests.

The subsequent rendering pass checked calm vertical rain while looking ahead, down and up, plus wind-driven rain after a half-turn. The cloud pass returned to the earlier translucent violet/red artwork with evolving shapes. All 53 regression tests still passed and browser shader/runtime logs were clear.

Cave-view correction: the Storm + Cave mouth check showed outdoor rain while listener exposure and local precipitation stayed zero; the deeper Cave view remained dry. Neighboring-column and elevation-independent particle-activity regressions bring the full suite to 55 passing tests.

## Cliff surf checks

In the weather survey, **Cliff overlook** frames a steep coast from 1,100 m altitude and about 2 km offshore. Compare **Clear** and **Storm**, allowing roughly a minute of simulation time for the existing sea-state smoothing to settle. Check approaching crests, intermittent foam rising on the rock, and the spreading wash between impacts. Also inspect the coast from the Mountain view and at ground level; a summit's own slope can obscure its cliff foot.

Cliff surf shares a periodic impact clock between the sea and terrain materials. Its height and spread follow the existing smoothed sea state. Flat shading, reflection rendering and mesh density are retained. A static 512-square RGBA float overview (4 MiB) extends coastal coverage beyond the moving 3 km shoreline map; it is built once from the generated world. The narrow surf band uses one additional landward height sample to distinguish a cliff above a shallow shelf from a beach. No render passes or particle systems are added.

Incoming rollers extend 360 m offshore, with approximately 58 m between crests and an 8.4-second period. Their height, distant lighting and foam use the same shoreward phase. Offshore chop fades back through that band to make the direction legible. Narrow white lips precede broader breaking foam inside the last 145 m; the foam thins behind each crest before the shared beach/cliff impact. At close range the actual triangle normals retain the faceted look; at distance the roller slope supplements the analytic water normal. These dimensions are artistic pacing choices. Compare frames two seconds apart to check that crests advance toward land rather than merely pulsing at the waterline.

This is a stylized impact approximation, not a fluid solver: there is no airborne spray volume, wave reflection/refraction around headlands, or propagation of individual offshore crests into the shore cycle. The coarse overview also omits narrow river channels outside the detailed maps. Regression tests cover shoreline distances, elevated lake exclusion, uniform maps and bounded overview memory.
