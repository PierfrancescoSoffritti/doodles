# Vegetation in the world

Open `index.html?plants=bell-reed&seed=ondine-ossia` or `index.html?plants=veil-willow&seed=ondine-ossia`. The field guide includes both plants alongside the fauna. Select a species to enter and visit it, **Visit another patch/willow** to move through distinct sites, and **Explore here** to walk around it. Visits preserve the seed in the URL and keep the selected plant in the streamed set.

Bell reeds grow in compact families along river margins and lake shores: a mature or weathered adult with two or three individually rooted companions, including smaller young plants. Companion scales range from 43–82% of the adult, with the young form shorter again, within 3.5–7.5 world units. Bell reeds are excluded from sea-level coastal banks (water height ≤2 and coastal habitat strength >0.35), including companion plants. Inland banks and elevated lakes retain their normal scale. Complete patches stream together. Willows stand farther up sheltered damp banks, with ribbons, sprays and veils. Roots use detailed terrain heights; placement rejects submerged ground, steep slopes, foamy water and cave openings. Willow trunks have collision. Existing terrain vegetation remains, so density and overlap with that vegetation are still useful things to tune in this first world pass.

Reed stems have one external hook: the internal bulb support begins beneath the crown. Bulbs have a soft white resting glow like a dim miniature lamp, warming to gold and brightening during interaction. **N**, a held N, or a world click sends the player's existing note; reeds within the full visible blip radius answer with synchronized bell notes and warm light. Clicking a husk selects that exact flower for the first answer in 45 ms and enlarges the cursor. The player note carries that target through audio dispatch; other nearby patches begin slightly later. Untargeted leads and following replies use a seeded varied order favoring flowers that have sounded less often, independent of distance. Repeated taps preserve travelling replies, with spacing shaped by the tapping rhythm. Each sounding flower gives a visible springy nod and bends its stem slightly; overlapping nods preserve the current pose. A charged blip makes every flower in all nearby families light and nod together, overriding clicked-flower ordering. Their pitches form a simultaneous chord of up to six shared voices, so a large patch does not create an oscillator burst. Small flowers sound higher and settle sooner; larger flowers have lower, longer voices. There is no recovery lockout or blanket flash. Replies use the current world scale, spatial panning and shared mixer, with six plant bell voices; when full, an older tail fades out over 30 ms so the new reply still sounds. Plant replies cannot start another response. Walking close bends reeds away from the actual player. Both species follow world wind; surface plants sleep while underground.

About one in five willows carries two mirror pendants. Aim at a pendant to enlarge the reticle and light its rim, then click to swing it and play the same `Conductor.mirrorTouch` sound and ripple as a standing mirror. Stable, slightly enlarged pick volumes follow the moving pendants; each click is accepted. While the field guide is open, the reticle follows the mouse over the world and target clicks work without entering mouse-look. Normal play still uses center-screen gaze, and touch keeps its existing gesture controls. Pendants and existing mirror landmarks share a global limit of two active planar reflections. This pass adds willow pendants; it does not yet migrate the original standing mirror encounters or their discovery progression.

World plants load within 600 units and remain resident until 700 units. Every valid nearby patch stays in place: there is no nearest-three replacement. Full-size silhouettes thin with a coverage fade between 380 and 520 units, well beyond interaction range; they never grow or shrink. During travel, new rigs build one per frame ahead of the visible boundary. Forced field-guide visits populate the destination immediately. Distant rigs remain static and only plants within 180 units animate; nearby plants and active replies update every frame, others at 10 Hz.

Each complete patch or willow uses one ordinary draw with static vertex buffers. A small floating-point texture supplies per-part transforms, normals, colors, roughness, metalness and emissive values to the GPU. Flower light and nods retain their individual timing without rewriting all vertices or assigning a draw call to each flower material. Conservative bounds enable frustum culling. Reflective pendants keep the existing shared two-reflection budget. Retiring specimens release geometry, transform textures, materials, reflectors and colliders. There are no per-bulb point lights.

A Node microbenchmark of three mature reed families (two small companions each, 600 updates after warmup) measured 108 → 3 ordinary draws, 779,328 → 57,744 uploaded bytes per animation update, and about 0.68 → 0.074 ms of CPU update time on the development machine. These are isolated plant measurements, not whole-game frame-rate guarantees.

World regressions cover deterministic habitat placement and forms, terrain rejection, spacing, stable distance streaming, static GPU vertex buffers, finite batched geometry (including zero-delta startup), collider cleanup, nearby player-note filtering, bounded conversational phrases, clicked-flower priority, fair varied replies, continuous nods, flower-size voices, synchronized sound and light, voice replacement, full note reach, companion heights, stable pendant picking, matching mirror sound and real-player brushing. Browser checks cover both species, repeat visits and player-note replies in the full game.

Willow pendant clicks add a bounded impulse to a damped swing, preserving its current pose even on fast repeats. The existing mirror chime stays at the pendant; ground rings originate at the tree root. The clicked pendant uses the fauna silhouette echo pass. Repeated clicks let the current outline travel finish before a fresh echo begins, and streamed removal releases its mask resources.

Click routing resolves a target once on release, including the last hovered moving pendant. A pendant click consumes that gesture and plays only its mirror chime; its audio note is excluded from general world-note ripples. Empty-space clicks and N retain the player blip.
