# Distance transitions — investigation, 10 September 2026

The first implementation pass is now complete. See [implemented changes, measured results and reproduction instructions](performance/distance-transitions-2026-09-10/README.md). The text below records the initial investigation and the remaining options.

The abruptness can be reduced while keeping the existing streaming distances and geometry budgets. Small vegetation needs a transition before its chunk is removed; large trees need continuity of silhouette; water needs continuity between geometric waves and the shading of its coarse surface. There is also an ocean boundary mismatch worth fixing first.

The initial investigation used the working tree based on `f335e23`; no game code had been changed at that point. Runtime checks used an isolated Chromium/ANGLE Metal page, seed `umbra`, 1280 × 800, at 30 fps, after a ten-second warm-up. They established geometry and deletion behaviour, not a visual comparison or a performance benchmark. Existing river edits in the working tree were left intact. Raw observations are in [the initial runtime audit](performance/2026-09-10-distance-transitions.json).

| System | Current behaviour | Consequence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ordinary vegetation | 256-unit chunks, a 9 × 9 square, removed immediately when outside radius 4 | A moving rectangular edge, roughly 1,024–1,280 units away along its sides, farther at corners |
| Grass | Density falls toward about 30% between 50 and 320 units, then remains nonzero | Thinning does not hide the eventual chunk deletion |
| Giant trees | High and low detail share placement; low copies are zero-scaled whenever a near chunk exists | Whole chunks switch model immediately; the far layer also ends abruptly around 3 km along its nearest sides |
| Lakes and rivers | Detailed surface radius 260; wave displacement fades over the final 50 units; static surface continues across the continent | Water remains present and animated in shading, but the geometric wave pattern flattens over a narrow band |
| Ocean | Six rings with 2–64-unit cells; outer side at 4,096, flat horizon mesh outside | The displacement fade is configured for 5,200–7,600, beyond the side boundary |

Distances are game world units. The game deliberately enlarges the player and vegetation, so these do not correspond to ordinary human-scale viewing distances.

**Vegetation findings.** [Config.js](../js/core/Config.js) defines the chunk sizes and radii. [Terrain.js](../js/world/Terrain.js) queues a new square when the player crosses a 256-unit cell boundary; [Vegetation.js](../js/world/Vegetation.js), `update` and `removeChunk`, immediately removes the outgoing cells. In the isolated runtime page, advancing the vegetation manager's centre by one cell removed nine chunks in one update: 92 mesh objects containing 5,398 instanced items, plus merged line geometry. Those counts describe removed content, not necessarily visible plants or saved draw calls. The audit deliberately invoked the manager directly to isolate removal; it was not a filmed walking route.

The blade and tuft shaders already thin vegetation deterministically, but neither reaches zero at the streaming edge. The existing growth animation is a separate proximity effect: some plants exist as soon as a chunk loads, while others grow when approached. Changing that behaviour is unnecessary to address distant disappearance.

Start by extending the existing small-plant density/size envelope to zero inside the guaranteed loaded area. An initial tuning band could be 650–950 units, below the 1,024-unit minimum side coverage; this leaves some streaming margin. Use each plant's fixed base position and a stable per-plant seed, with different bands for grass, reeds and shrubs. Taper luminous tips and decorative lights too, or bloom will expose their last visible pixels. Keep this envelope independent of growth state. Late chunk installation still needs a short arrival fade, especially after rapid travel.

This uses existing vertices and materials and needs no extra render pass. Shader arithmetic is added; collapsing plants does not remove their vertex processing. Actual CPU/GPU savings would require later culling or compaction, which is separate work.

[TerrainMaterial.js](../js/world/TerrainMaterial.js) already samples the same habitat map as vegetation placement and darkens distant forests from 120–900 units. Reuse those samples to match distant meadow and reed-bed colour/coverage as individual plants disappear. Fading alone softens the edge but still leaves empty-looking ground; the terrain treatment supplies continuity. Colour on flat terrain cannot preserve a large tree's skyline.

**Large trees need a different treatment.** `refreshFar` in [Vegetation.js](../js/world/Vegetation.js) switches between matching high/low trees according to near-chunk existence. A bounded transition between these existing representations would preserve scale and shape better than shrinking trees into the ground. Keep low detail until high detail is installed, and use a fade factor shared by bark, leaves and attached lights. Base visibility on the player's position consistently in main, reflection and environment passes; the current `cameraPosition` changes between render cameras.

Dithered cross-fades are a candidate, but not free: both models render during overlap. Unity's [LOD documentation](https://docs.unity3d.com/2022.3/Documentation/Manual/class-LODGroup.html#transitions) describes this tradeoff and geometry morphing as an alternative. This renderer has no temporal antialiasing, so animated random dithering could shimmer; any dither must be checked in motion. A fixed overlap budget and earlier use of the existing low-detail model could fund the transition. Extending silhouettes beyond the current far radius with instanced billboards is a second-stage option, with additional placement, memory and fill cost; it is not required for the first pass.

**Fog is currently inconsistent.** Ordinary trees, grass and giant trees use Three.js exponential-squared fog (`main.js`, density 0.00045 in clear weather). Terrain and water use [FogGlsl.js](../js/world/FogGlsl.js), which depends on altitude. Straight ahead, the built-in fog blends only about 19–28% toward fog colour at the near vegetation side boundary. It cannot conceal removal there, particularly against the sky. Sharing the world's height-fog model would make vegetation belong to distant slopes, but is not itself a fade-to-invisibility mechanism. Dense global fog would obscure the long landscape views. Fog unification is a useful consistency improvement to assess after the direct transition fixes.

**Ocean mismatch.** [Water.js](../js/world/Water.js) describes `RIM = 8192` as the endpoint, but that is the full width. Runtime buffer bounds confirm the outer ring extends from −4096 to +4096, and the flat reflector's central hole meets that boundary. [SeaShader.js](../js/world/SeaShader.js) leaves displacement at full strength until radial distance 5200. The two surfaces therefore do not meet at the same displaced position along the side edges; even corners have not finished fading. This is a confirmed configuration mismatch, though its visibility depends on viewpoint, weather and wave phase.

Fade all displacement components to zero before the actual outer boundary. Derive the envelope from the snapped clipmap centre and square extent, with the same world-position rule for adjacent rings; do not merely change a comment or use independently computed fades per ring. Keep reflection scheduling and geometry unchanged. This should have approximately the existing rendering cost and deserves a focused boundary regression.

Ocean detail has another limitation: `vNrm` and `vJac` are computed at vertices and interpolated, while cell spacing doubles up to 64 units and wavelengths range from 4.3 to 64. The advertised distant analytic normal is thus not evaluated per pixel; coarse geometry undersamples the waves before interpolation. A distance blend at 700–1600 cannot recover detail that was already lost. The horizon mesh is coarser still. Keep only resolvable wave displacement on coarse meshes and transition remaining appearance into filtered shading. Adjacent rings must agree at shared vertices. Start with the existing shoreline shading and a small number of broad wave components; evaluating all six waves per distant fragment could materially increase GPU cost.

**Inland water.** [WaterShader.js](../js/world/WaterShader.js) retains advected noise, foam and optical shading on both near and far surfaces. The issue is not a complete loss of animation. Its normal, however, combines each mesh's geometric normal with procedural noise; the far shader does not preserve the slopes of the same river/lake waves that vanish geometrically. Transition toward those wave slopes in shading as displacement subsides, retaining the river's integrated travel phase. Extend the blend inward rather than enlarge the 260-unit detailed radius. Keep drifting foam on the same surface envelope. Separating geometric waves from shading detail is an established approach described in NVIDIA's [GPU Gems water chapter](https://developer.nvidia.com/gpugems/gpugems/part-i-natural-effects/chapter-1-effective-water-simulation-physical-models); the specific adaptation here still needs a measured prototype.

The shore-map tiers already blend into each other and a static island overview. Coastal foam already has pixel-footprint filtering; river drift particles fade from 168–240 units. Preserve those mechanisms. They are not equivalent to the unsoftened vegetation cutoff.

Recommended implementation order: correct the ocean envelope; taper small vegetation and match distant ground coverage; prototype the existing tree-model transition; then improve wave shading where coarse water still looks flat. For each stage, compare identical seeded paths at ground level and from an overlook, crossing boundaries in both directions and under clear/storm lighting. Include diagonal ocean views and 64-unit clipmap snaps, loading delays, reflections and teleports. Check p95 frame time, draw/triangle counts, streaming time and GPU duration where available. The existing [performance runner](../tests/performance/compare.cjs) supplies a starting point, but its CPU/submission measurements alone cannot establish GPU neutrality. Keep changes only if the measured cost fits the current budget.
