Sheltered reedbeds

A slow, edible river

Shallow fresh water, submerged plants and reliable footing. Quiet backwaters and broad vegetated banks.

A familiar grazing route

The family returns to the same patches. Finding the same family again should feel like visiting old neighbors.

Together, without ceremony

Two parents and one or two youngsters graze several paces apart. The youngsters stay near the adults; each animal keeps its own slow rhythm.

In the world, families inhabit shallow fresh water with safe footing. Each animal follows its own grazing rhythm within a shared river patch.

Two small family moments. Watch a youngster get distracted and catch up, or settle against a parent who quietly makes room. These previews isolate a parent and youngster so you can inspect the interaction. Choose a family in Form to begin.

Contentment, not vigilance

Like a very old elephant: it has a routine, and your arrival rarely changes it.

Weight before movement

One foot lifts, reaches and settles. Then the body follows. Long pauses between mouthfuls.

Almost indifferent

It gradually changes course around a nearby visitor, keeping its unhurried rhythm.

Family resemblance

Males have broader, flatter husks, cool earthy colors and gently curved tusks. Females have warmer colors and an arched back. Youngsters are paler and rounder, with shorter steps; young males grow small tusks.

A lifetime on its surface

Softened edges, pale wear and slight asymmetry. Older, larger individuals have deeper voices.

A small family

A father and mother share familiar river margins with one or two youngsters. Each has room to graze; the youngsters follow a little later. Affection shows through proximity and quiet contact rumbles.

The mystery stays

Its feeding is understandable. Its senses, age and thoughts are harder to place.
